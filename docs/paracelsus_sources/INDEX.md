## Content Summary

### Paracelsus (1493-1541) Overview
- Born in Einsiedeln, Switzerland; died in Salzburg, Austria
- Physician, medical theorist, philosopher of nature, theologian, mystic, alchemist
- Responded to early Reformation crisis of authority (1520s)
- Proposed medical reform parallel to Luther's theological reform
- Integrated medicine, alchemy, magic, and theology
- Controversial in lifetime; famous posthumously
- 20+ works identified (mostly post-mortem editions)
- Central concept: "Theory" (theorica) roaming across disciplines

### Primary Scholars
- **Andrew Weeks**: Text-centered historicism; crisis of authority thesis; 1997, 2007, 2024 works
- **Charles Webster**: Medicine-magic-eschatology integration; Oxford emeritus
- **Didier Kahn**: Cosmology/meteorology editor; 2024 recent edition
- **Kurt Goldammer**: Theological tradition; most valued by Weeks

### Major Issues
1. Paracelsus as anomaly (neither Luther nor Faust; autonomous thinker)
2. Integration of science and religion (not separate domains)
3. Concentration of key writings in 1520s-30s (not spread across lifetime)
4. Textual authenticity (post-mortem editions; authentication crucial)
5. Alchemy's role (recent trend: central, not peripheral)

### Historiographical Innovation
- **Weeks's Text-Centered Historicism**: Let writings speak in their context; evaluate by internal coherence
- **"Theory" (Theorica) Framework**: Bypasses false science/religion dichotomy
- **Crisis of Authority Model**: 1520s Reformation affects all intellectual domains
