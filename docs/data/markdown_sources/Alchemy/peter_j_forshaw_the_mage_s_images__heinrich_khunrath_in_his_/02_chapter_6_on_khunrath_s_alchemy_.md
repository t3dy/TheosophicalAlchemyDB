10 1591 A Powerful Patron in Bohemia
Regularly visited in the capital of his domains at Český Krumlov in South
Bohemia by the Holy Roman Emperor Rudolf II (1552–1612) and other heads of
state,157 Vilém Rožmberk (commonly Germanicised as Wilhelm von Rosenberg,
1535–1592), belonged to one of the oldest and most powerful noble families in
Bohemia, with a court second only to that of Rudolf.158 Appointed Supreme
Burgrave at Prague Castle in 1576, he was Rudolf’s trusted right-hand-man in
all things Bohemian,159 receiving the highest accolade of Habsburg chivalry,
the order of the Golden Fleece, together with the Emperor in 1585.160 Though
a Catholic and supporter of the Jesuits, funding their new college completed
in Český Krumlov in 1588, Vilém expressed great tolerance towards those of
other religious persuasions, no less so than in the fact that two of his four
wives were Protestant.161 Like Rudolf, he was fascinated by occult philosophy
and his court was a magnet for alchemists, with him employing over 50,162
including the Bohemian nobleman Bavor Rodovský (1526–c.1592), Daniel
Prandtner von Prandt, the Silesian Christoff von Hirschenberg (fl. 1574–92),
Anton Michael von Ebersbach (d. 1593), the Italian Claudius Syrrus,163 the

11 1592 The Ancient Arabian King and Sage Zebel, Prague
The next sign we have of Khunrath is the following year with the publication,
at the ‘will of great men, and the request of excellent and learned men’,175
of his personal copy of an astrological manuscript made by the historian
and astronomer Johann Carion (1499–1537), which had been in the library
of the Electors of Brandenburg until it was presented as a gift by the late
Elector August of Saxony to a faithful minister, one of Khunrath’s relatives.176
Published as a parallel-text translation in Latin and German, it bears the title,
The Most Accurate Observations of the Ancient Arabian King and Sage Zebel
on the Interpretations of Various Occurrences, both External and Internal, or
Unexpected, Rare, and Unusual Events, according to the Movement of the Moon
through the Twelve Signs of the Heavenly Zodiac. From the title page (Fig. 1.14)
and preface we discover that Khunrath was then residing in Prague and
still acting as Vilém’s Medicus Ordinarius; indeed, the preface is dated the
23 August 1592, just eight days before Vilém’s death on the 31st.

The Zebel treatise itself is a fairly standard work listing the physical affects
to be expected when the Moon progresses through the various signs of the
zodiac. Being the mistress of moisture,182 the Moon was considered important
for medicine, particularly in connection with catarchic or elective astrology,
as she is the planet with the swiftest motion, her changeability due to the fact
that in one cycle she only spends about 2½ days in each zodiac sign, pass-
ing through them all in 28 days.183 The work is attributed to the ‘Arabic King
and Sage’ Zebel, who may tentatively be identified as the ninth-century Jewish
astrologer and mathematician Sahl ibn Bishr (c.786–845), author of Liber Zahel
de judiciis (Zahel’s Book on Judgments), an astrological work that provides one
of the earliest references to alchemy.184

Zebel discusses how God speaks with man in various ways, through the stars,
as well as through his animal, vegetable and mineral sublunar creations as let-

in order to communicate future events.186 As in his graduation Theses we find
Khunrath asserting the same notion of natural signs revealing knowledge to
the wise, combined with the effort to show the Christian orthodoxy of occult
studies. We also find the strong political statement that God not only communi-
cates with princes but also with men of lesser status, a frank assertion of equal
worth in the eyes of God which was to be a repeated refrain in later works.187
Even in this first publication there is already the sense that Khunrath’s occult
and egalitarian religio-political beliefs roused stiff opposition, with references
to calumniators and carping critics at the end of the preface.188

Khunrath’,190 it should be remembered that astrology had long been an ancil-
