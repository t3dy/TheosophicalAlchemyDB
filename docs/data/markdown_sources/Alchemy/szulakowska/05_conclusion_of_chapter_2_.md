Conclusion of Chapter 2:
In this chapter, Szulakowska demonstrates that alchemical interpretations of the Eucharist were
deeply intertwined with the theological debates of the Reformation. Alchemists like Khunrath
and Maier rejected the Catholic doctrine of transubstantiation, aligning themselves with
Protestant critiques of sacramental theology. Instead, they viewed the Eucharist as a symbolic
act that mirrored the alchemical process of spiritual transformation. By reimagining Christ as the
philosopher’s stone, alchemists elevated their work to the level of a sacred ritual, where the
transformation of matter became a metaphor for the transformation of the soul.
Through her analysis of alchemical texts and illustrations, Szulakowska reveals the deep
connections between alchemical and theological thought during this period, showing how
alchemists used the language and symbolism of the Eucharist to express their own esoteric
ideas. The Eucharist, in their view, was not just a religious sacrament but a powerful symbol of
the alchemical process, where Christ’s body represented the ultimate substance that could
transmute base matter into spiritual gold.
This chapter lays the groundwork for understanding the broader relationship between alchemy
and Protestant eschatology, which Szulakowska explores in subsequent chapters. The
alchemical Eucharist becomes a key metaphor for the alchemists’ vision of spiritual and material
transformation, reflecting their engagement with both religious and esoteric traditions.
