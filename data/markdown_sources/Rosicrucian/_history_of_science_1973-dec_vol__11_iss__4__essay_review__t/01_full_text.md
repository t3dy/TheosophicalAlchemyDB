Another subject requiring investigation is that of good and bad angels.
Angels as a source of intellectual enlightment play a dominant role among
the 'Rosicrucians',1 as one can see by looking at the works of Andreae,
Fludd or Comenius, and they were of course already very active in their
main Renaissance sources, Dee and Paracelsus, especially as interpreted
by one ofhistranslators, Jacques Gohorry (= Leo Suavius). Asonewould
expect, Dr Yates quite rightly takes them seriously, and does not try to
explainthem awayasmetaphorsforsomething else. But they remain for us
puzzling, because we do not know what kind of actual experience they
represented for these seventeenth century thinkers. One might get near
to solving this problem by trying to discover what means they used to get
into contact with angels-whether, on the one hand, they used a medium,
or a crystal, or Cabalistic numerical or verbal formulae of conjuration, or
whether, on the other, it was by internal prayer and meditation that they
sought angelic illumination. In the first case, they would be practising
the kind of magicthat weknow Dee used, trying to produce external effects
(sights or sounds) which today we can explain only as frauds or hallucina
tions, and laying themselves open to accusations of having converse with
devils. In the second, they would be aiming at a purely subjective effect,
which they maywellhaveachieved,and doing nothing that the most strictly
orthodox Christian could object to. One way into this subject would be
to followup the references to such magic in Keith Thomas's monumental
and fascinating book, Religion andthedecline ofmagic. The question is of
considerable historical importance, because the possible unorthodoxy or
inadvertant diabolism of dealings with angels was a major force working
against the Rosicrucians, as is shown by the publication of their ancestor
Dee's 'Spiritual Diary' by Meric Casaubon, with wholly destructive in
tentions and results. For it is important to remember that at this period,
since all Christians believedin angels, II people disapproved of conversewith
angelsnot becauseits practitionerswereconsidered superstitiousor deluded,
butbecausetheywerethoughttobediabolic-whichismuchmoredangerous.
