# Szulakowska

## Section: Chapter 5 of The Sacrificial Body and the Day of Doom, titled The Alchemical Las

Chapter 5 of The Sacrificial Body and the Day of Doom, titled The Alchemical Last Judgment,
examines the apocalyptic and eschatological themes in alchemical literature and imagery,
focusing on how alchemists interpreted the Last Judgment as a metaphor for the final stage of
alchemical work. Urszula Szulakowska argues that just as the Last Judgment in Christian
eschatology represents the ultimate purification of the soul and the world, so too did alchemists
view their work as a form of purification that culminated in a final, divine reckoning.
Main Argument of Chapter 5:
Szulakowska’s argument centers on the idea that the Last Judgment, a central theme in
Christian eschatology, was appropriated by alchemists to describe the culmination of the
alchemical process. The Last Judgment symbolized the final purification and transformation of
both matter and soul, where the alchemist’s work was seen as participating in the cosmic drama
of redemption and renewal. Alchemists believed that their work mirrored the divine judgment
that would bring about the ultimate reconciliation of spirit and matter, reflecting the Christian
narrative of the end of the world and the resurrection of the dead.
Key Themes in Chapter 5:
1. The Alchemical Day of Judgment: Szulakowska explores how alchemical texts and
illustrations depicted the Last Judgment as a metaphor for the completion of the Great
Work. The Last Judgment was seen as the moment when the impurities of the world
would be burned away, leaving only the purest elements to be resurrected. Similarly,
alchemists believed that their work involved the purification of matter, where the
philosopher’s stone symbolized the final stage of this process, akin to the soul’s
resurrection at the Last Judgment.
She writes: “For alchemists, the Last Judgment represented the ultimate purification of
both the cosmos and the soul. The Great Work was seen as a microcosmic reflection of
this divine judgment, where the impurities of base matter were burned away, leaving only
the perfected philosopher’s stone, the alchemical equivalent of the resurrected soul” (p.
110).
2. Alchemical Depictions of Apocalypse: Alchemical texts often included apocalyptic
imagery, depicting scenes of cosmic destruction and rebirth. Szulakowska analyzes
illustrations from works like Heinrich Khunrath’s Amphitheatrum Sapientiae Aeternae and
Michael Maier’s Atalanta Fugiens, where the Last Judgment is depicted as both a literal
event and a metaphor for the alchemical process. In these illustrations, the destruction of
the world is shown alongside the resurrection of purified matter, symbolizing the
alchemical belief in the cyclical nature of death and rebirth.
She notes: “Alchemical imagery of the apocalypse frequently depicted scenes of fire and

[Page 63]
destruction, where the old world was consumed, and a new, purified world emerged.
This imagery reflected the alchemical belief that the end of the world was not only a
cosmic event but also a personal, spiritual transformation” (p. 112).
3. Judgment and the Philosopher’s Stone: Szulakowska argues that the philosopher’s
stone was seen as the key to surviving the alchemical Last Judgment. Just as the
righteous souls would be resurrected and saved at the Last Judgment, the philosopher’s
stone represented the perfected matter that would emerge from the alchemical fires. The
stone was seen as a form of salvation, both material and spiritual, where the alchemist’s
work was justified by the final transmutation of base matter into gold.
She writes: “The philosopher’s stone was viewed as the ultimate reward for the
alchemist’s work, the perfected substance that would survive the fires of the Last
Judgment. Just as the souls of the righteous would be saved, so too would the
philosopher’s stone emerge from the alchemical process as a symbol of divine
perfection” (p. 115).
4. The Role of Fire in Alchemical Eschatology: Fire plays a central role in both
alchemical and Christian eschatological thought. Szulakowska explains that in alchemy,
fire was seen as the purifying element that burned away the impurities of matter, just as
the fires of the Last Judgment would purify the world. Alchemists believed that their work
involved controlling and harnessing this fire, using it to transmute base matter into gold
and preparing their own souls for the final judgment.
She notes: “Fire was the central element in alchemical eschatology, symbolizing both
destruction and purification. The alchemist’s control of fire mirrored the divine fires of the
Last Judgment, which would cleanse the world and resurrect the purified souls of the
righteous” (p. 118).
5. Alchemical Salvation and Eschatology: Szulakowska concludes the chapter by
emphasizing the eschatological dimension of alchemical thought. Alchemists saw their
work as participating in the divine plan of salvation, where the purification of matter was
a reflection of the purification of the soul. The philosopher’s stone, as the ultimate
product of the alchemical process, represented the perfected state of both matter and
soul, a symbol of salvation that paralleled the resurrection of the dead at the Last
Judgment.
She writes: “For the alchemists, salvation was not only a spiritual event but also a
material one, where the resurrection of matter in the form of the philosopher’s stone
mirrored the resurrection of the soul at the Last Judgment. The alchemical process was
thus seen as a microcosm of divine eschatology, where the alchemist’s work participated
in the cosmic drama of redemption” (p. 120).
Methodological Approach in Chapter 5:
As in previous chapters, Szulakowska employs a multidisciplinary approach, combining art
historical analysis with religious and intellectual history. She draws on a wide range of
alchemical texts and illustrations, focusing on how apocalyptic and eschatological themes were
used to describe the culmination of the alchemical work. Her analysis places these alchemical
depictions of the Last Judgment in dialogue with Protestant and Catholic eschatological thought,

[Page 64]
showing how alchemists used the imagery of divine judgment to articulate their own vision of
material and spiritual transformation.
Szulakowska also engages with the broader religious and cultural context of the Protestant
Reformation, particularly the apocalyptic fervor that gripped many religious thinkers of the time.
She shows how alchemists participated in this eschatological discourse, using the language and
imagery of the Last Judgment to describe their own work of purification and transformation.
