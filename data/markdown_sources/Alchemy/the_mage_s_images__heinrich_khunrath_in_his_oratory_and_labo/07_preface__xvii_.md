to Jeiunium or fasting with prayer could itself be seen as a corporeal form of
penitential prayer. King David is cited as an example of one who, although a
sinner, adulterer and murderer, nevertheless ‘always raised himself again by
repentance’,906 and the theosopher is urged to recite psalms every day after the

kindles the light of Chastity, sharpens the Intellect, elevates the Mind,

46 10) Similitudo (Likeness)
In De Trinitate (On the Trinity), Augustine explains that the mind resembles
what it knows, and as a result, ‘We are like God inasmuch as we know Him.’1003
The conviction that knowledge consists in likeness between the knower and
the object known was often expressed in the definition of truth as ‘the adequa-
tion of the intellect with its object.’1004 In the context of Khunrath’s sources,
such an idea is voiced in Corpus Hermeticum XI:
You must think of god in this way, as having everything – the cosmos,
himself <the> universe – like thoughts within himself. Thus, unless you
make yourself equal to god, you cannot understand god; like is under-
stood by like.1005
A similar sentiment is expressed by the hermit Pelagius, just mentioned above,
where in the same text he writes that the angel ‘does not establish contact save
by way of a certain intellectual likeness, namely, the human mind shaking off
earthly, carnal and corruptible things, ascending with a pure intellect, is con-
joined through a relationship of an angelic nature’.1006
This state is the apogee of all those meditations and contemplations, the
bringing together (according to the Corpus Hermeticum) of ‘all the sensa-
tions of what has been made, of fire and water, dry and wet’ with the advice
