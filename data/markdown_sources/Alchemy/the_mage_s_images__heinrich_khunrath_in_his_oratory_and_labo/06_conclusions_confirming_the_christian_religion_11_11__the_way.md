For Khunrath this love finds its perfect object in the love of Christ as the
Wisdom of God. He is quick to remind the reader that Philosophy after all
means the ‘Love of Wisdom’,865 and any philosopher who seeks true wisdom
will receive a reciprocal response from Wisdom.866 ‘Love and you will be loved,’
Khunrath exclaims, borrowing from Cyprian, and assures his reader that ‘The
love of someone living in true faith is capable of All Things.’867 Indeed no con-
junction can take place ‘unless love arises, and holy desire.’868 Echoing Thomas
à Kempis, Khunrath exhorts his reader to imitate Christ as the Archetype,869
the practice of Love being realised in Caritas (Charity) and Eleemosyna (alms-
giving), in the act of love towards one’s neighbour, which is the true law of the
New Testament.870
Otto Casmann writes that such love is the justification for self-knowledge,
which becomes a form of Sancta Philautia, Holy Self-Love, expressed in the act
by which man descends into and examines himself and discerns his virtues
and vices.871 As such it differs greatly from the Philautia or egotistical self-love
of pride, the source of all evils, and that love of the impure world, so thor-
oughly condemned by Khunrath,872 as by Erasmus before him.873 Rather than
the Philautia that manifests as insincere munificence,874 Khunrath’s aim is for
the genuine care of the needy, in the giving of alms, food, clothing and medical
assistance, notably, not just to Christians but to all mankind.875 This love also
expresses itself in the exercise of perfect equity (ἐπιεικεια – epieikeia) towards
accidentally, as happens in the death of the kiss, of which it is written: Precious in the sight
of the Lord is the death of his saints.’

41 6) Oratio (Prayer)
‘The door of Wisdom is truly the fear of GOD and assiduous prayers.’892
Oratio or Prayer takes many forms. In De Modo Orandi (On the Way of
Praying), Hugh of Saint Victor wrote of three kinds of prayer: the first of these,
precatio, being humble and devout supplication, without determination or
petition (petitio), or ‘undetermined narration’ (narratio) for a determined
petition, and insinuatio or ‘indicating what is wanted without petition but
solely through narration’. ‘Pure Prayer’ (pura oratio), on the other hand, is
when ‘out of an abundance of devotion the mind is so inflamed that, about to
make a request to God, it is so transformed before the magnitude of his love
