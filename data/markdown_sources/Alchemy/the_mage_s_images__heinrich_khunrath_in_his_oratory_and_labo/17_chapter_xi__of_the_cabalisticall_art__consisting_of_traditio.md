24 Sigillum Dei: Khunrath’s Christian-Cabalist Seal of God
Nowhere does Khunrath’s interest in Jewish Kabbalah more clearly reveal itself
than in the Amphitheatre’s First Circular Figure. As mentioned in Chapter 2,
Raphael Patai points out that one ‘has the impression of seeing a complex
Jewish emblem written in Hebrew’: a central figure from which radiate eight
concentric rings, five in Hebrew letters, forming ‘a veritable brief anthology
of important quotations and names of Jewish religious significance.’431 This
figure is a compendium of some of the loci communes cabalistici, as it were, in
particular the most powerful Divine Names,432 which Arndt in the Judicium
considers to be the prime concern of Cabala,433 the choice of the Name most
suitable for the ‘operation’ being essential for the successful accomplish-
ment of the adept’s goal of understanding and influencing the cosmological
structure of the Sephiroth.434 Let us look at this figure in more detail, moving
inwards from the circumference to the centre.

25 1. Circumference: Tetraktys and Decalogue
As on the Amphitheatre’s title page, we see the Pythagorean Tetraktys (Fig. 4.31),
a symbol of the graduated descent of God’s ‘Light’ into matter.435 Reuchlin has
much to say about it, calling it the ‘source of everlastingness.’436 It displays ‘all
the gods and the gods’ powers, and is the source of perpetual nature dwelling
in our minds’, and its fourfold quality manifests in various ways as the four
elements in Chaos, the four seasons in the Heavens, the four winds in the Air,
the harmonic proportions of music, and so forth.437 The Tetraktys’s four levels
admit multiple readings, including the descending hierarchy of the four ele-
ments (Fire, Air, Water, Earth), the four magnitudes (point, line, plane, solid),
the forms of simple bodies (pyramid, octahedron, icosahedron, and cube), or
the faculties of judgement (intellect, science, opinion, sense).438 Occurring in
Dov Cooperman (ed.), Jewish Thought in the Sixteenth Century (Cambridge, Mass: Harvard
University Press, 1983), 188–200, at 207 ‘The prayer of those versed in esoteric wisdom is
superior … they know which prayer suits which particular emanation.’ Cf. Gikatilla, Gates
of Light, 13 ‘If one does not know how to concentrate on the very Name which is the key to
the answer to his request, then who is to blame if the request is not granted?’

Finally, as the foundation, we have the most powerful Jewish divine name
הוהי Yod Heh Vav Heh, the Tetragrammaton. Its letters represent a numerical
progression: Yod as the first letter is both 10 and 1. The second letter He has the
alphanumeric value of 5 and contains the numbers 2 + 3. Reuchlin explains
that ‘the Binary and first Ternary form the Quinary, which produced by the
supreme light [is] the first ray of the intellectual World’.453 The third letter Vav,
with the value of 6, functions grammatically as the coordinating conjunction
“and” – as in Esch va Maim (Fire and Water) – and as such Reuchlin explains
that it links the numbers 1 + 2 + 3.454 After a brief passage praising Man as a
great miracle, after the fashion of the Hermetic Asclepius, quoted by Pico in
his Oration, and his ability to transform into the nature of God, to know dae-
mons, and be conjoined with divinity, and how the rational soul is the medium
between the visible and invisible worlds, Reuchlin tells us that the final He
represents the midpoint of the denary, and as such symbolises the ‘reduction
of man to divinity’.455
