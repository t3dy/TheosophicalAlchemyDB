It is evident, however, that Khunrath’s ten grades were unacceptable to
more orthodox contemporaries. In Examen philosophiae novae (1615), Libavius
predictably declares the ‘Gradus cognitionis magica [sic]’ (magical Grades of
cognition) to be un-Christian.653
Although the three main divisions of the soul (vegetative, sensitive, intel-
lective) represent a hierarchical ‘ladder of nature’ or ‘great chain of being’,
defined metaphysically according to their increasing abstraction from matter
and theologically according to their increasing closeness to God, in scholastic
philosophy the highest functions nevertheless relied on input derived from the
senses for the generation of knowledge, in accordance with the Aristotelian
dictum that all knowledge is derived from the senses.654 Thus it is that the
first two grades in Khunrath’s list (Obiectum, Medium) concern the external
object of perception and that which had become the basis of the philosoph-
ical understanding of sensation, the sense image or species. Although ‘the
sense object cannot be received by the sense in its essence on account of its
materiality it produces an image which the sense can receive and by which it
can be perceived.’655 This image is the sensible species, an immaterial quality
impressed on the surrounding medium like a seal on soft wax, which ‘resem-
bles the object as a portrait does the sitter […] Every sense object constantly

From Khunrath’s occasional reference to the idea of species it may be that

they reached the appropriate Outer Sense organ (eye, ear, nose, tongue, skin)

organs of Inner Sense located within the brain (Common Sense and Imagination

tricle; Memory in the posterior ventricle). Once in the organs of Inner Sense,
the species were impressed on an internal medium, the vaporous spiritus filling
the sense organs and the nerves.660

The Memory stores both those images derived from the Outer Senses as well
as the phantasmata and the reactions of Estimation. It differs from Phantasy
in the fact that it recognises its contents as part of past experience.676 Robert
Markus provides a clear sense of the importance of Memory, which contains
‘everything the mind is capable of knowing or thinking about’, being ‘the whole
potential knowledge of an individual mind at any one time.’ It is not, moreover,
simply Epimethean in its functioning, but has a Promethean quality, for placed
within memory are ‘the forms by which the mind knows in potentia all that it
knows before it knows anything.’677
Next, we come to the faculties of the Intellective soul, the grouping of Ratio,
Intellectus and Mens, or Reason, Intellect and Mind.678 The generally accepted
definition for Ratio is the faculty of the soul that coordinates the images sup-
plied by the Inner Senses according to the rules of logic, and becomes involved
with the experiences, desires and needs of the body as transmitted by the per-
ceptual faculties. Man’s unique position in creation arises from the fact that
while he shares the faculties of the lower, sensitive soul with the animals and
his Intellect with the Intellectus Divinus, he shares his Reason with nothing in
the universe. Reason is exclusively human, a discursive and reflective faculty
capable of turning in whichever direction it chooses, which enables man to
escape from the three deterministic systems of providence, fate, and nature
that control his intellect, imagination, and body.679
