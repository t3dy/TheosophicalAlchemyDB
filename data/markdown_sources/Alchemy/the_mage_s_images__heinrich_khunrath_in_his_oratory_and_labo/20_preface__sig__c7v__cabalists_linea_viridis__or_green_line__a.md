As has already been mentioned in Chapter Two, Dorn is the author of
Monarchia Physica, also known as Monarchia Triadis, in Unitate (1577), a short
work written ‘in defense of the Paracelsian principles’.741 There he provides
geometrical representations of what he means by the Unary (Fig. 4.60), Binary

Ternary’, i.e. the Paracelsian Tria Prima or Three Principles of Mercury, Sulphur
and Salt (Fig. 4.63), plus images of the ‘first three principles of true medicine,
proved by mystical measures’ (Fig. 4.64) and ‘of the principles of false medi-
cine, disproved by mystical measures’.742
An immediate source for such ideas (if not the images themselves) is
most likely Reuchlin, who equates his Christian version of Kabbalah with
Pythagorean mysticism, in particular the conceptual links he sees between the
most famous Hebrew name for God, the four-letter Tetragrammaton, YHVH
and what he calls the ‘first principle of the Pythagorean system,’ the ‘one great
thing above all things’, the Tetraktys.743

A third possible source for Khunrath’s Pythagorean-sounding phrase must
be Agrippa’s mentor the abbot Trithemius, whose qualifications for being a
magus have already been compared to Della Porta’s magician and Khunrath’s

Martin Ruland’s Lexicon of Alchemy (1612), where he states that ‘Trithemius’s

By Unity is understood that which Hermes explains in other words, and by
a similitude taken from Genesis. ‘What (he says) is above’, and so forth.752
For the Binary is denoted by Hermes by above and below. Trithemius says
‘Let the Binary be rejected and the Ternary will be convertible to [the
simplicity of] Unity.’ So does Hermes in other words, ‘For accomplishing
(he says) the miracles of One thing.’ It should particularly be noted here
that Unity is understood by the two men in two different ways. Hermes
numbers Unity, as we have said, by below and above, while Trithemius by
the constitution of the first number from the Unary. Unity is measured by
both of them in a very different way, the former by the reduction of above
and below to a likeness located in the ‘miracle’;753 the latter by rejection
of the Binary, and conversion of the Ternary into the Unary. In a wonder-
ful way both indeed arrive at the same conclusion: therefore, Unity itself
is natural and yet divisible, or rather numerable in the Binary, and the
Ternary is revertible into another Unity, which is called the second Unity,
beyond which it is not permitted to go.754
