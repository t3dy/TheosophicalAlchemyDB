## Section: preface to his work, the author includes an acrostic, informing the observant

preface to his work, the author includes an acrostic, informing the observant
reader that ‘I, Elchanan, son of Rabbi Menachem, nowadays known as Paul
of Prague, … etc’.289 It is tempting to see this as the inspiration behind the
Consilium Dei acrostic that Khunrath includes with his portrait engraving in
the Amphitheatre.
Khunrath is careful to emphasise, moreover, that whatever scriptural words
are used, be they Hebrew, Greek or Latin, they should be understood in more
than their literal etymological sense and he reminds the reader that the ‘Book,

that the reader must scrutinise every last detail of the scriptures in order to
discover their hidden truths, Khunrath quotes the Lutheran Johannes Olearius
(1546–1623), Doctor in Theology and Professor of Hebrew Language at the
Julian Academy, Helmstadt, eulogising the Cabala, with the lines,

A simple example of Notariacon is found in the unfolding of the Latin word for

in Khunrath’s inclusion of the alchemical word for Mercury of the Philosophers,
Azoth in Circular Figure 3, where it appears on the body of the Avis Hermetis
(Bird of Hermes). Highly significant for the alchemists, like ELIXEIR the word
AZOTH is another polyglot word, considered to be formed of the first and last
letters of the three matrix languages – Latin a and z, Greek α (alpha) and ω
(omega), and Hebrew א (aleph) and ת (tau), – and encapsulates the message of
the whole diagram: the transformation of Prima materia into Ultima materia,
the ‘vile’ into the ‘blessed.’388 The sense of universal signification is reinforced
by the fact that the central letter ‘O’ of Azoth appears to be Dee’s hieroglyphic
Monad.389 The best-known example of Notariacon in an alchemical context is
the VITRIOL acrostic: ‘Visita interiorem terrae rectificando invenies occultum
lapidem’ (Visit the interior of the earth and by rectifying you shall find the hid-
den stone), as found in, for example, Basil Valentine’s Azoth (1613).390
