The Artis Cabalisticae contained the already-mentioned works by Reuchlin
and the Latin translation of Gikatilla’s Shaʿarei ʾOrah (Gates of Light), by
one of Reuchlin’s friends,232 Paolo Ricci, which originally appeared in 1516.233
Pistorius’s volume also includes the most famous of Ricci’s Kabbalistic works,
the four-part religio-philosophical synthesis of Kabbalistic and Christian
sources, De Caelesti Agricultura (On Celestial Agriculture, 1541), the fourth
book of which is an introduction to the Kabbalah in a series of fifty theorems.234
Ricci discovered in Kabbalah the mysteries of the Trinity, the eternal gener-
ation of the Son of God, redemption through the passion and blood of the
Messiah, and his resurrection. He was widely-read in Hebrew sources and,
with the zeal of a convert, published a series of short tracts under the title
Sal Foederis (Salt of the Covenant) in 1507, intended to defend Christianity
against the calumnies of the Jews. In 1514 Ricci became physician to Emperor
Maximilian I (1459–1519), for whom in 1519 he prepared a new Latin transla-
tion, with commentary, of the Talmud.235
