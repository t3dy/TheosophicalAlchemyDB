Text: (5.19) The ignorant ones are those who do not know the 74
consensus of opinion of the sages in their statements and their symbols.
They do not try to obtain knowledge from the sources recommended by the
people (sages) and they do not follow their advice and do not heed their
warnings. Rather he (the ignorant one) pretends—according to his own
desires and the conflicts [coming from] his devilish imagination—that he
has access to the hidden secret of God. He acts in the world according to
the ideal of manhood and the sister of prophethood without either refraining,
nor obedience, nor readiness, nor knowledge, nor work. He relies on the
outward meaning of what the sages say—although the sages warned
against this and forbade it—assuming in his wrong thinking that the sages
spoke to him directly, revealing the secrets of God without symbols.
Therefore he is confused and weary as long as he lives, to the detriment
of his wealth, religion, dignity and life. (5.31)
In this passage there is a subtle criticism of the ignorant ones who 75
continue to live «according to the pure ideal of manhood and the sister of
prophethood», that is according to the real goal of alchemy. But as they
only stay with the tangible, visible values, they fall into imitating the sages
and live without the required devotion. Hence a subtle inflation creeps in,
turning those persons into victims of their own role play. They become
deluded «by their devilish imagination» into thinking that they can have
direct «access to the hidden secret of God» and act accordingly as if they
would know the truth. Therefore they no longer need to ask humbly and
continuously the symbols of their dreams and fantasies what they might
point to. The result of this «wrong thinking» is confusion, poverty, and the
destruction of true human values.
Like alchemical authors before and after him, Ibn Umail warns of this 76
«wrong thinking» of the ignorant people who are unable to see that the secrets
of God and the words of the sages are coming to us in the form of symbols,
and have therefore to be understood accordingly. When looking now at the
Hall ar-rumüz we also must investigate with utmost care the significance of

77 Ibn Umail also points out that literal understanding of alchemical
texts is just as misleading as is a way of life that corresponds outwardly to
the «ideal of manhood and the sister of prophethood», yet is dominated «by
desires and conflicts». This will never lead «to the hidden secret of God»
or, as we would say today, to the inner meaning of any overwhelming
strong desire. Desires or drives are by their very nature conservative and
repetitive. No transformation is possible on that biological level. Only by
following the advice of the sages, namely by seriously pondering on the
possible meaning of such suspended desires, can the goal of the work be
attained. In alchemical language, this is expressed by closing such an over­
whelming fire into a retort and then observing what comes out of it (see
fig. 24). This is indeed the main difficulty in relation to the archetypal
dimension of the psyche: in order not to become inundated or possessed by a
constellated archetype, a process of ongoing Auseinandersetzung is needed.
This requires a continuous careful suspension of any strong emotion, followed
by an intense pondering over the hidden meaning of the symbolic expression
of such forceful desires, urges or compulsions. To Ibn Umail’s way of
thinking this would mean the ability to discover «the secrets of God», or
God’s will behind these symbols, and later reconnecting these insights with
one's life. An ego-centred attitude, however, will only lead to a pale imitation
of a life that corresponds to the ethics required for the alchemical work.
