active imagination This is a psychological technique of an intro­
verted visualizing and looking at emotions
that was rediscovered by C. G. Jung. It played
a central role in alchemy, for instance as
«colloquium interium cum aliquid, qui non
videtur» (inner dialogue with somebody who
is not seen, [outside]), as was said by a Latin
alchemist. Instead of just living out an incom­
prehensible fascinating, overwhelming
emotion in the outer world, the adept suspends
such an instinctual drive and puts the desired
object in a vessel (see e.g. fig. 24). Then the
adept lets the mental images which come
along with such an emotion gradually unfold.
It is important not to remain just a passive
observer, but to ask questions (colloquium!)
and to ponder over the meaning of the emerging
symbols and insights (see e.g. §27).
