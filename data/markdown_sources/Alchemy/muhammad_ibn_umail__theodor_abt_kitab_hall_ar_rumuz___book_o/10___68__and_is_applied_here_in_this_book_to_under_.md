(“dream girl”), or in the irrationalities of a

archetype Archetypes are common organizing factors in
the unconscious psyche, visible only indirectly
by their effects in the form of archetypal
pictures or symbols (->). Archetypes are the
inherited unconscious organizers, whereas
symbols are their not-inherited, temporarily
und individually determined contents.
Archetypes can be compared with the axial
system of a crystal, which is immanent in the
crystalline structure in the mother liquid,
although it has no material existence of its
own (see page 68). C. G. Jung reintroduced
this psychological term.
aurora Latin term for the dawning of the sun.
complex Complexes are part of the psyche that can
interfere with the intentions of the will of the
ego (-► ), and thus disturb the conscious
performance, producing disturbances of memory
and blockages in the flow of associations. They
can temporarily obsess consciousness, or
influence speech and action in an uncontrol­
lable way. Their autonomy, and their presence
in all human beings led C. G. Jung to the
hypothesis of a collective unconscious (-*) and
the archetypes (—), among other facts.
coniunctio Latin term for union.
consciousness A sphere or part of the human being that
emerges from the unconscious (-*). It is related
to the state of being awake, to conscious thinking,
observation of feeling, etc. In childhood,

ego The centre and subject of all conscious personal

extraversion Attitude of consciousness (—) characterized by an

four functions of The adaptation of the individuals to their envi­
consciousness ronment can take place in four basic forms or
psychological functions: The sensation regis­
ters consciously the facts, the feeling function
establishes the value order, the thinking func­
tion looks at the structure and logical order, and
the intuition appears to be a kind of perception
via the unconscious (->) leading to hunches.
god-image An image of the highest value or of something
that is stronger than the human will or under­
standing, which traditionally was attributed to
God. From the psychological point of view,
such an image is a symbol (-♦ ) of the self (-+),
of psychic wholeness.
individuation Denotes a process of self-development in
which the individual human being becomes
what he or she is meant to be in his or her
uniqueness, becoming oneself.
introversion Attitude of consciousness (-► ) characterized by an
orientation going towards inner psychic contents.
prima materia Latin term for the first substance with which
the alchemical work has to start. It is a symbol
(->) for the adept him- or herself who is able to
put the “unacceptable fascinating other” of an
overwhelming emotion into the alchemical
vessel for careful and continuous observation.

self The self is not only the centre of the psyche
but also the whole circumference which
embraces both consciousness and the uncon­
scious; it is the centre of this totality, just as
the ego (->■ ) is the centre of consciousness. It
corresponds to the second body of Ibn Umail
that is created out of the first body or the prima
materia (-► ). C. G. Jung termed this central
archetype (-*■ ) of order the self.
shadow The usually inferior or non-valued part of the
personality. It is the sum of all personal and
collective psychic elements which, because of
their incompatibility with the chosen conscious
attitude, are denied expression in life. But it
also displays a number of good qualities, such
as normal instincts, appropriate reactions, realis­
tic insights and creative impulses. The shadow
behaves compensatorily to consciousness.
subtle body Different oral traditions as well as cultures in
the East and the West are familiar with the idea
of a subtle body. In modem depth psychology,
the hypothesis of the existence of a subtle body
was developed from the fact that there is an
unknown gap between our body-matter and the
psyche, as we know nothing about the interac­
tion between body-matter and psyche. We can
only indirectly draw conclusions about it. In
depth psychology, the assumption of the exis­
tence of a subtle body between psyche and mat­
ter considers that this symbolic body reaches
into matter on the one hand, and to have psy­
chic features on the other. Therefore the subtle
body is considered as being able to mediate
between these opposites of psyche and matter.
Being subtler than matter, it is supposed to be
beyond time and space, and therefore beyond
decay.
In alchemy, the subtle body has to be
symbolically created, and given birth to by the

[Page 400]

alchemical work. This subtle body is named by
Ibn Umail the second body that came into being
from the so-called first body. The alchemical
process of purification or distillation frees the
human ego more and more from false attach­
ments to material reality. Since the subtle body
originates in the first body, it carries the indi­
vidual features of the adept. As potentiality, this
subtle body exists in the unconscious for every­
body, but only when brought to consciousness
by the work is it able to develop the mediating
or healing effect between psyche and matter, the
upper and the lower in a human being. That is
why this second body is named elixir or remedy.
symbol They are visible manifestations of an activated
archetype (->■ ), As an archetypal image it is the
best possible expression of that still unknown
content beyond consciousness. As already
pointed out by alchemists, the meaning of
symbols can only be understood by collecting
necessary associations ( ► amplification and § 65).
unconscious The contents of the unconscious consist of still
completely unknown, repressed or forgotten
contents. It can also be called the inner night-
world. It is the common or collective back­
ground of consciousness of all human beings,
and it has a structure and an autonomy that can
be observed like outer objects. That is why
C. G. Jung also called this independent second
psychic system “the objective psyche”.
unus mundus Latin alchemical term used by the alchemist
Dorneus (16th Century) for the unity of the
psycho-physical reality.
unio mystica Latin term for the enigmatic conjunction of
the human soul with god.
