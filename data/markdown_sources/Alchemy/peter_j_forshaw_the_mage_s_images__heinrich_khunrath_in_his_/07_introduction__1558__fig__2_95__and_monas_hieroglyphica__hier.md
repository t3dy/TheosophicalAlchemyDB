Introduction, 1558, Fig. 2.95) and Monas Hieroglyphica (Hieroglyphic Monad,
1564, Fig. 2.96).527 The same has been argued about the glyph substituted for
the central letter O of the alchemical word AZOTH on the breast of Hermes’
Bird in Circular Figure 3 (Fig. 2.97).528
In the Monas Hieroglyphica, Dee explains that ‘Mercury becomes parent
and king of all the planets when perfected by a stable pointed hook’, i.e. the
Zodiac sign for Aries.529 He shows how the characters for all seven planets
in the Ptolemaic cosmos and related seven metals are included in his hiero-
glyph, thus uniting Astronomia Superior (Astrology) with Astronomia Inferior
(Alchemy), or the ‘Above’ and ‘Below’ of the Emerald Tablet of Hermes. Given
the large collection of Paracelsian works in Dee’s library, it seems reason-
able to suggest that on one level of interpretation his hieroglyph represents
Paracelsus’s alloy of all metals, his electrum magicum, used in the fashioning
of magical mirrors and necromantic bells.530 On an alchemical level, the hier-
oglyph is a combination of the planetary sign for ‘watery’ Mercury and the
zodiac symbol for the first fire sign Aries, a metonym for ‘fiery’ Sulphur and as
such the two conjoined represent the fiery-water, quintessence or ‘heaven’ of
the alchemists.531

30 Changing Perspective
The Alchemical Citadel is a curious combination of bird’s-eye view and sin-
gle point perspective, for the adept moves from a fairly flat two-dimensional
landscape outside into a more pronounced three-dimensional perspective
within the Citadel itself; though it displays none of the skill of De Vries’ work
in the Oratory-Laboratory.564 Here the angle of perspective and relative size
of the objects varies so much from one side of the engraving to the other that
there is little sense of naturalistic representation, far more of the picture being
a tableau of symbolic images. Töllner and Szulakowska have both noted the
mixture of a flat, map-like or aerial view on the outside of the Citadel and
three-dimensionality at its centre.565

33 The Sequence of the Images
Although there is a clear sequence for the circular engravings in the 1595
Amphitheatre (1. Christ-Cruciform; 2. Adam-Androgyne; 3. Rebis; 4. Oratory-
Laboratory), no two surviving copies of the 1609 Amphitheatre appear to have
been bound with the same format, many in fact lack the full complement of
images, and there are no internal indications of whether any formal sequence
was ever intended for the full set of engravings, nor any clear set of names for
the images.628 Indeed, there seems to have been confusion about this at the
very time of printing, for on the final page of the 1609 edition the publisher
Wilhelm Anton adds an apologetic note ‘To the Candid Reader’:
At the end of the exposition of each step, candid Reader, by error these
