EPHEMERAL ARCHITECTURE
Such manifestations of ephemeral architecture as triumphal entries
allowed larger "entities" such as cities to exist as "emblems," if only for
brief periods of time. Larger entities such as a street, square or even a
whole city are used in a ceremonial action that may perhaps be compared
to the use by a priest of his church in the liturgy. The motion through space
with its symbolic fixed points is analogous. Procession, entry or progress
through a palace, church, or city could also carry an extended reference to
passage through the heavens.
As a secularized liturgy such carefully orchestrated movement is a
ritual that can connect territorial or dynastie daims to other and different
events, persons and places, which thus become united into a larger image
or "frame 11 for the person celebrated in the procession. This frame becomes
the ideal image of the patron, host, or more often the powerful and
honoured guest. In the case of entries they may celebrate metaphorically a
victory in war or diplomacy, or a victory over temporality in the case of
funerary monuments. At a lower secular level the ritual movement may be
a celebration of the patron's entry into a higher social order through wealth
or aristocratie marriage. Marriage, birth and baptism, and death are the
occasion for such ritual celebrations which may include the constructions of
ephemeral architecture.
These processions, progresses and entries were highly ritualistic. In
a sense they represent the passage between an actual society and an ideal
society. As passages particular attention is given to points of entry and exit,
whether the structure be a palace, church or city. Alberti recommends pay
ing 11 attention to marking major threshold within a building or its larger
analogue, the city. 114 A threshold marks an opening which is at the same
time a closing that both mediates and separates. Thus for Sebastiano Serlio
the design of portals requires "the display of eschatons, emblems and other
markers of social position. 115 Ritual movement links various points of entry
